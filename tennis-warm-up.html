Essential warm-up routines before every match

By Sasha AceFebruary 21, 20254 min read

Before you even pick up your racket, give your body a chance to prepare. Tennis asks you to accelerate, stop, change direction and rotate — sometimes all within a few seconds. A warm-up should gradually introduce those demands, rather than take energy away from your match.

You do not need a complicated routine. Around 10–15 minutes of purposeful movement can help you arrive at the baseline feeling warmer, more mobile and more focused. Adapt the pace to your fitness, the weather and how your body feels that day.

1. Start by raising your temperature

Spend three to five minutes walking briskly, jogging gently or skipping at an easy pace. Follow with relaxed side steps along the baseline. Keep your shoulders loose and your breathing comfortable: this is preparation, not a fitness test.

On a cold morning, allow a little longer for this stage. If you have been sitting for several hours, build up especially gradually instead of going straight into fast sprints.

2. Move through your range of motion

Use controlled, dynamic movements to prepare your ankles, hips, upper back and shoulders. Start small and increase the range only as you feel comfortable.

  • Ankles and calves: gentle calf raises and ankle circles to prepare for quick steps.
  • Hips and legs: walking lunges and shallow side lunges, keeping your knees comfortably aligned.
  • Upper body: relaxed arm circles and standing torso rotations without forcing the movement.
Coach’s tip: A warm-up should feel increasingly comfortable. Sharp pain is a reason to stop, not something to stretch through. If you have an injury or a medical condition, ask a qualified professional how to adapt your routine.

3. Wake up your tennis footwork

Add a few short movement patterns: a split step, two or three side steps, then a balanced recovery. Practise moving forward and back as well as sideways. Start at half speed and build toward the pace you expect to use on court.

Include several shadow forehands and backhands. Turn your shoulders, step into position and finish in balance. The aim is to connect your feet with your swing, not to make the biggest or fastest movement possible.

4. Build up with the ball

Begin with mini-tennis inside the service boxes. Use a relaxed grip and aim for clean contact. Gradually move back, then add volleys and overheads if appropriate. Start your serves gently, focusing on rhythm and placement before increasing the pace.

Use these first rallies to notice the conditions: the bounce, the wind and how the surface feels under your feet. This turns your warm-up into useful preparation for the decisions you will make during play.

A simple routine you can repeat

  1. Three to five minutes of easy movement.
  2. Three minutes of dynamic mobility.
  3. Two minutes of footwork and shadow swings.
  4. A few minutes of progressive hitting and serving.

Finish feeling ready rather than tired. A repeatable routine also gives you a calm starting point: whatever happened earlier in your day, these few minutes are your transition into tennis.
