Master your serve: tips for more power and accuracy

By Sasha AceMarch 10, 20254 min read

The serve is the one shot you begin entirely on your own terms. That can make it feel both exciting and frustrating: you have time to prepare, yet a small change in rhythm can send the ball somewhere unexpected. The solution is usually not to swing harder, but to make the movement more repeatable.

Build control first. A relaxed serve that regularly finds its target is more useful than an occasional powerful one. Once your balance, toss and contact become dependable, you can increase pace without losing the shape of the shot.

1. Begin with a comfortable setup

Stand behind the baseline in a balanced position, with your body turned partly sideways to the net. Your exact foot position can vary; what matters is that you feel stable and can move through the action without twisting awkwardly.

A continental grip is a common starting point for learning a full serve. Hold the racket loosely enough that your wrist and forearm can move naturally. If the grip feels unfamiliar, practise gentle overhead motions with a coach before adding speed.

2. Make the toss predictable

A changing toss forces a changing swing. Release the ball smoothly from your fingertips with a relatively straight tossing arm, rather than flicking it from your wrist. Aim for a contact position you can reach comfortably, generally above and slightly in front of you.

Practise the toss without hitting. Notice where the ball rises and falls, and whether you can stay balanced underneath it. If a toss drifts too far, catch it and reset — chasing a poor toss is not a habit worth rehearsing.

Coach’s tip: Separate toss practice from serving practice. A few minutes of calm repetition makes it easier to notice the release, without the distraction of trying to hit the ball.

3. Find rhythm before power

Think of the serve as a connected movement rather than an arm doing all the work. Your legs, trunk, shoulder and racket contribute in sequence. Start with a compact motion and an easy swing, then develop a fuller action as your coordination improves.

Aim for a smooth acceleration into contact and a natural follow-through. Avoid deliberately snapping your wrist or forcing your shoulder into a position that feels uncomfortable. Pain is a signal to stop and get appropriate advice.

4. Practise placement with clear targets

Divide the service box into broad zones: wide, body and centre. Start with generous targets rather than trying to clip the lines. Choose your target before each serve and judge the result by both direction and consistency.

  • First round: serve ten balls at a comfortable pace, aiming for the middle of the box.
  • Second round: alternate between two broad target zones.
  • Third round: play a first-serve and second-serve sequence, prioritising a reliable second ball.

Record how many serves land in, not just the fastest one. Increase pace only when you can maintain your balance and repeat your chosen direction.

5. Give every serve the same preparation

Use a short routine: choose the target, settle your feet, breathe and begin. It gives you something familiar to return to when a match becomes tense. Between attempts, change one thing at a time rather than rebuilding your entire technique after a miss.

Short, focused sessions are often more productive than a large bucket of rushed serves. Warm up first, take breaks and finish before fatigue turns good repetitions into forced movements.
